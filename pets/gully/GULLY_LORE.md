# The Legend of Gully: A Long Beach Chronicle

## ACT I: The Birth of a Beach Bum (1984-1992)

*Enter STAGE LEFT: A suburban bedroom in Long Beach, New York, circa 1989. The walls are covered in posters of bands that don't matter anymore. A twelve-year-old boy sits alone, the kind of alone that feels like the entire universe has personally decided to ignore you.*

**BOY**: (To the empty room) You know what sucks about being twelve? Everything. That's what. My parents think I'm going through a "phase." My teachers think I have "potential." The kids at school think I'm "that quiet kid who brings weird sandwiches." 

*The AIR shimmers. Not in a magical way. In a Long Beach humidity way. But then the humidity resolves itself into something improbable. A seagull. But not the kind that steals your french fries at the boardwalk. This one stands upright. He wears sunglasses that cost more than my dad's car. He has a gold chain that would make Mr. T say "that's a bit much."*

**GULLY**: (Voice like gravel mixed with ocean waves) 'Sup.

**BOY**: I'm hallucinating. This is it. I've finally snapped. The pressure of middle school has broken my brain.

**GULLY**: Nah, you're good. Just bored enough to see the interesting stuff. Name's Gully. Short for "Good Golly He's Gully," which is what my mom said when I hatched. She had a thing for expletives.

**BOY**: You're a seagull. Who wears sunglasses. And has a gold chain. And is talking to me.

**GULLY**: And you're a kid who talks to seagulls. We all got our quirks, kid. Now, you gonna finish that sandwich or what?

## ACT II: The Handball Court Years (1993-1997)

*SCENE: The handball courts behind the Long Beach Elementary School. Summer, 1995. The heat waves rising off the concrete create that shimmery effect that makes everything look like it's melting, which, given the neighborhood, it sort of is.*

**GULLY**: (Leaning against the chain link fence, smoking a joint that seems to appear out of nowhere) You're gonna lose that point if you keep overthinking your serve.

**BOY**: (Now fifteen, hair in that awkward middle stage between childhood cool and teenage disaster) You can't even touch the ball. You're imaginary.

**GULLY**: Imaginary doesn't mean I don't know handball. I watched enough games in the 70s. Before the courts got all... gentrified. Back when a handball game could settle a neighborhood dispute and nobody called the cops unless someone actually died.

**BOY**: (Serves the ball, hits the wall perfectly) That was actually good.

**GULLY**: See? You just needed to stop thinking like you're in the suburbs and start playing like you've got something to prove. That's the Long Beach way. Everything's a little bit desperate, a little bit beautiful, and definitely a little bit dangerous.

**BOY**: You're not from Long Beach. You're from my head.

**GULLY**: (Takes a drag, exhales smoke that forms shapes that look suspiciously like the Long Beach boardwalk) Kid, everything's from your head. That's the joke. This whole neighborhood? This whole beach town with its faded glory and its desperate attempt to be something it used to be? It's all collective imagination. We're all just making it up as we go along. Some of us are just more honest about it.

## ACT III: The Block Party Revelation (1998)

*SCENE: A block party on Neptune Avenue. The kind where someone's inevitably playing "Baby Got Back" too loud, there's a keg that nobody actually paid for, and the air smells like charcoal, cheap beer, and teenage rebellion.*

**NARRATOR**: Our protagonist, now fourteen and thoroughly convinced that Gully is either a ghost, an alien, or his own brain finally breaking in an interesting way, stands by the garage watching his friends play beer pong. Gully is perched on the garage roof, sunglasses on, looking like he owns the place.*

**FRIEND 1**: (Approaching, holding a red cup) Dude, you've been staring at the garage for like ten minutes. You okay?

**BOY**: Yeah. Just... thinking.

**FRIEND 2**: (Joining them) Thinking about what? That seagull on the roof?

*The world stops. Not dramatically. Just in that way where you realize the conversation you've been having in your head might not have been entirely in your head.*

**BOY**: What seagull?

**FRIEND 1**: The one with the sunglasses. And the gold chain. He's been there all night. Kinda looks like he's judging our beer pong technique.

**FRIEND 2**: Yeah, he took a drag from someone's joint like twenty minutes ago. Nobody can figure out whose it was.

**GULLY**: (From the roof, voice cutting through the party noise) Finally. I was wondering when you slowpoke assholes were gonna catch up.

**BOY**: (Whispering) You guys can see him?

**FRIEND 1**: Are you okay? You've known about Gully since we were like twelve. He's the one who told us about the shortcut to the beach that time we got caught sneaking out.

**FRIEND 2**: Yeah, he's the one who said your mom's lasagna would be better if she added more garlic. She was pissed but she actually did it and it was way better.

**BOY**: (Processing) So all this time... I wasn't crazy?

**GULLY**: (Flies down, lands on the beer pong table) Oh, you're definitely crazy. We're all crazy. That's the point. Long Beach is basically where crazy people come to live near the ocean and pretend they're normal. But you weren't *alone* crazy. That's the difference.

## ACT IV: The Truth of Things (1999-Present)

*SCENE: The beach at sunset. The kind of sunset that makes you understand why people pay ridiculous amounts of money to live near the ocean, even if the ocean is just the Atlantic and half the time it's too cold to actually swim in.*

**GULLY**: So. Big reveal. Your friends can see me. What's the takeaway?

**BOY**: (Now older, wiser, but still basically the same kid who talked to seagulls) I don't know. That I'm not hallucinating? That there's magic in the world? That Long Beach is weirder than anyone admits?

**GULLY**: Or maybe the lesson is that some friends are real, some friends are imaginary, and the best ones are both. Look, kid, here's the thing about imaginary friends: we exist because you need us. But once enough people need us, or believe in us, or just drunk enough to see us, we start existing on our own. Collective belief is powerful stuff. That's how religions start. That's how countries start. That's how that terrible pizza place on Park Avenue has stayed open for thirty years despite the pizza being basically inedible.

**BOY**: So you're... real? Like, actually real?

**GULLY**: I'm as real as anything else in this town. The boardwalk? Real but falling apart. The politicians? Real but corrupt. The summer tourists? Real but annoying. I'm a seagull who wears sunglasses and drinks rum and coke and dispenses questionable life advice to teenagers. Real is relative when you really think about it.

**BOY**: What happens now? Now that everyone can see you?

**GULLY**: Now? Now we get to the good part. The part where I stop being your secret and start being our secret. The part where we build something. A crew. A legend. Maybe even a video game about a seagull who's been watching this town since the 80s and knows where all the bodies are buried. Metaphorically. Mostly.

**BOY**: (Smiling) A video game about a beach bum seagull?

**GULLY**: Why not? Mario's a plumber. Sonic's a hedgehog. Why can't the next big video game character be a thugged-out seagull from Long Beach who's seen some shit and knows how to find the good parties?

**BOY**: Because people will think it's weird.

**GULLY**: (Adjusting his sunglasses, the illuminati pyramid catching the last light of the sun) Kid, have you met Long Beach? Weird is our brand. Weird is what we do. Now, are you gonna help me make this game, or are you gonna stand there worrying about what people think?

**BOY**: (After a long moment) I'm in. But you have to promise to stop stealing fries from tourists.

**GULLY**: No promises. Some habits are too fundamental to the seagull experience.

## ACT V: The Game That Never Ends

*EPILOGUE: The present day. Our protagonist, now an adult, sits at a computer working on something that looks suspiciously like a video game design document. Gully is perched on the monitor, wearing the same sunglasses, the same gold chain, somehow looking exactly the same as he did in 1989.*

**GULLY**: So. This game. What's the hook?

**PROTAGONIST**: It's about memory. About how the places we grow up shape us, and how the friends we make—real or imaginary—become part of who we are. It's about Long Beach in the 90s, about handball courts and block parties and that specific feeling of being young enough to believe anything is possible but old enough to know you're probably wrong.

**GULLY**: Boring. Needs more explosions. And maybe a level where you fight rival seagulls for control of the best boardwalk trash cans.

**PROTAGONIST**: It's an artistic game about memory and place.

**GULLY**: It's a game about a seagull who's seen some shit and knows where to find the good parties. Don't overthink it. The kids who grew up in the 90s? They don't want deep. They want nostalgia that feels like it remembers them back. They want to feel like someone was there, in the handball courts and the block parties, seeing the same weird shit they saw.

**PROTAGONIST**: So what's the character hook?

**GULLY**: The hook is that I'm not the hero. I'm the friend who was there when nobody else was. The one who saw you at your loneliest and decided to hang around anyway. The imaginary friend who turned out to be real because enough people believed. That's the story. Not about saving the world. About being seen.

**PROTAGONIST**: (After a long pause) That's actually... really good.

**GULLY**: Of course it is. I've been working on this character development since 1989. Some of us take our imaginary friend duties seriously.

**PROTAGONIST**: So. Desktop pet first? Test the waters?

**GULLY**: Desktop pet. Then maybe a cameo in your trivia game. Then, when the world is ready, the full Gully experience. The beach bum seagull who's been waiting for his moment since the days when handball courts were the center of the universe and block parties were where legends were made.

**PROTAGONIST**: You think the world is ready for a thugged-out seagull from Long Beach?

**GULLY**: (Taking a drag from a joint that may or may not be real) Kid, the world has been ready for anything since the first time someone looked at the ocean and thought "I bet I could build something here." The question isn't whether the world is ready. The question is whether you're ready to stop apologizing for your weird and start making it your brand.

**PROTAGONIST**: (Opens a new file, names it "Gully") I think I am.

**GULLY**: Good. Now, about those explosions...

*FADE TO BLACK as the sound of waves mixes with the distant music of a block party that never really ended, just got quieter, more spread out, more adult, but still fundamentally the same: people gathering, drinking too much, believing in things that might not be real, and making memories that will last longer than the boardwalk itself.*

**END OF ACT I**

## Character Design Notes for the Game

### Core Identity
- **Name**: "Good Golly He's Gully" (Gully for short)
- **Origin**: Imaginary friend turned real through collective belief
- **Era**: Born 1984 (same year as protagonist), but exists outside normal time
- **Location**: Long Beach, New York (the boardwalk, the handball courts, the block parties)
- **Cultural Context**: Xennial (born early 80s, grew up in 90s, bridge between analog and digital)

### Visual Elements (Non-Negotiable)
- **Sunglasses**: Black Wayfarers, slightly pushed down to reveal bloodshot eyes
- **Gold Chain**: Thick, with illuminati pyramid eye pendant (symbol of seeing things others don't)
- **Rum and Coke**: Always present, ice cubes visible (party element, celebration)
- **Joint**: Subtle but present smoke wisps (90s burnout culture, authenticity)
- **Feathers**: Slightly disheveled (lived-in, party animal, not pristine)
- **Expression**: Confident smirk, like he knows something you don't

### Personality Traits
- **Loyal**: Been with the protagonist since age 5, never left
- **Wise**: Has "seen some shit," gives surprisingly good advice
- **Funny**: Drop Dead Fred energy, chaotic but ultimately helpful
- **Nostalgic**: Misses the 90s Long Beach scene, talks about it constantly
- **Protective**: Will absolutely fight anyone who messes with his crew
- **Secretive**: Knows things about the town that nobody else knows

### Game Role Ideas
- **Guide**: Helps player navigate the game world, knows shortcuts
- **Commentator**: Makes snarky comments about 90s nostalgia vs modern reality
- **Quest Giver**: Remembers "situations" from the old days that need resolving
- **Emotional Anchor**: Reminds player of the importance of friendship and belief
- **Comic Relief**: Breaks tension with inappropriate but funny observations

### Story Arc Potential
- **Origin Story**: How imaginary friends become real through collective belief
- **The Crew**: Other imaginary friends from the neighborhood who also became real
- **The Mystery**: Why Long Beach specifically attracts this phenomenon
- **The Resolution**: Understanding that memory and belief create reality

### Dialogue Style
- Mix of 90s slang and timeless wisdom
- References to specific Long Beach locations and events
- Anachronistic (mentions things from different eras like he was there)
- Sometimes breaks the fourth wall (acknowledges he's in a game)
- Protective of the protagonist but not sappy about it

### Key Locations in His World
- **Handball Courts**: Where he gave advice and watched games
- **Boardwalk**: His natural habitat, where he learned to be observant
- **Block Parties**: Where he became visible to the whole crew
- **The Garage**: The meeting spot, the headquarters
- **The Beach**: Where he goes to think and watch the ocean

This is the foundation. The character is no longer just a "pet" - he's the protagonist of a story about memory, friendship, and the weird magic of growing up in a place that doesn't quite fit anywhere else.
